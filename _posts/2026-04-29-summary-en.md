## [New 'Behavioral Timescale' Plasticity Rewires Brain After Single Experience](https://www.quantamagazine.org/a-new-type-of-neuroplasticity-rewires-the-brain-after-a-single-experience-20260424/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Scientists have identified a new type of synaptic plasticity called behavioral timescale synaptic plasticity (BTSP), which enables the brain to rewire its connections after just a single experience. This mechanism allows for rapid, one-shot learning in the hippocampus, fundamentally changing our understanding of how memories form. This discovery provides a concrete biological mechanism for one-shot learning, which has profound implications for understanding how episodic memories are formed and could inspire new, more efficient algorithms for artificial intelligence and machine learning systems. It bridges a key gap between observed learning behavior and the underlying neural circuit changes. BTSP operates on a timescale of seconds, which aligns with the duration of behavioral experiences, and its effect on synaptic weight depends on the prior state of the synapse. This form of plasticity was observed in hippocampal place cells in mice, providing a mechanism for creating conjunctive memories that integrate information over time.

Behavioral timescale synaptic plasticity (BTSP) is a newly characterized form that can induce significant synaptic changes in a single trial, making it a prime candidate for the neural basis of rapid, experience-dependent learning and memory formation.
